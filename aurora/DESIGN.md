# Aurora Design Language

## Purpose

Aurora reduces thinking, increases confidence, and presents truth beautifully.
It is a decision language, not a visual style.

Every screen must answer:

1. Where am I?
2. What matters now?
3. Why does it matter?
4. What should I do next?
5. Can I trust this?

If one answer is unclear, the screen is incomplete.

## Decision Hierarchy

Aurora presents information progressively:

### Layer 1 — Answer

The immediate verdict, state, or best next action. It must be useful without
opening another view.

### Layer 2 — Reasons

The few strongest factors supporting or weakening the answer. Reasons must come
from real source data, never generic confidence copy.

### Layer 3 — Evidence

Matchups, splits, weather, history, provenance, freshness, and uncertainty.

### Layer 4 — Deep Research

Complete expert detail. This layer is available on demand and never shown first.

## Composition

- One primary question per surface.
- One obvious primary action.
- Use typography and spacing before borders or effects.
- Prefer short evidence stacks over dense dashboard grids.
- Keep confirmed, projected, stale, unavailable, and blocked states explicit.
- Use empty and error states to explain what is missing and what the user can do.
- Mobile layouts are designed at narrow width, not compressed from desktop.

## Visual Character

Aurora is calm, precise, restrained, and premium. Premium means legibility,
craft, speed, and predictable behavior. It does not mean extra glow, gradients,
glass, animation, or decorative density.

## Trust Rules

- Never invent odds, markets, consensus, confidence, verification, or activity.
- Never use payment as proof of identity or quality.
- Show provenance and freshness near the claim they qualify.
- Use plain language for uncertainty.
- Preserve the exact meaning of upstream intelligence and resolution states.

## Responsive Rules

- Design and verify the critical journey at 390×844.
- No horizontal page overflow.
- Primary controls use at least 44×44 CSS pixels; prefer 48 pixels on mobile.
- Keep the immediate answer and next action above deep research.
- Tables require a deliberate small-screen alternative or safe horizontal region.

## Accessibility

Accessibility is part of premium quality. All interactions require keyboard
access, visible focus, semantic names, readable contrast, reduced-motion
behavior, and non-color state cues. Graphs require text equivalents.
